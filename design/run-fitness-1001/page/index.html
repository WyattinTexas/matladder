FOOTWORK · THE ROAD 2

The run screen now works the way the fitness app does: a live map with your dot and your route, one big start and pause button, distance, calories, pace, and a voice that calls each mile with your pace. Plank has 2:00 and 3:00 timers. Box breathing has an endless mode on the ∞ button.

“We need it to visually look just like the fitness app experience when you're running, so you can see where you are on the map. Able to start and stop it so easily, see how far you've gone, the calories… it needs to tell you when you reach a mile and what your pace is.” — your note, October 1

THE RUN, ON THE PHONE

This clip is the real app in the iPhone simulator, fed a simulated run around Lady Bird Lake. Nothing in it is a mock-up: the location comes through the phone's own location service, the map follows the dot, the route draws behind it.

  • Start: one green button, then 3 · 2 · 1 (tap to skip).
  • While you run: the map on top, the time in yellow, then distance, active calories, pace and average pace. Tap the map to make it big.
  • Stop: one big PAUSE. Paused shows RESUME and END. END takes two taps so a stray thumb cannot end a run.
  • Each mile: a banner and the voice: “Mile three. Pace, eight forty-two. Average pace, eight thirty-nine. Time, twenty-five fifty-seven.”
  • After: the workout page with your route colored by pace, calories, splits, and your group rank.

EVERY SCREEN

Swipe sideways. The first shots are from the simulator; the ones with a 5.6 mile run use the same app fed a full recorded-style run so the numbers are realistic.

THE MILE MOMENT

The banner drops in for six seconds with the mile's pace, your average pace and the time. A chime plays, then the phone's own voice says the line. In the simulator the voice call was confirmed spoken to the end by the phone's speech engine (the simulator's volume stays at zero, so there is no sound in this clip).

The simulated runner here moves at about 5:30 per mile so the clip reaches a mile in under six minutes.
